# Composition Patterns

8 base composition types. Each image should use one clearly.

---

## 1. Workflow / Process

**Use when:** The article describes a step-by-step process or pipeline.

**Structure:** Left-to-right or top-to-bottom sequence of 3–5 stages. Xiaohei moves through or operates the stages.

**Key rule:** Show the *transition* between stages, not just the stages themselves. Xiaohei should be at a transition point.

---

## 2. System / Modules

**Use when:** The article describes components or parts of a system working together.

**Structure:** Loosely arranged modules or zones with connectors. Xiaohei operates or monitors the whole or a key connection.

**Key rule:** Don't draw a formal architecture diagram. Use physical objects (boxes, tubes, levers) not UI components.

---

## 3. Before / After Contrast

**Use when:** The article compares two states: wrong vs. right, old vs. new, problem vs. solution.

**Structure:** Split canvas (left / right, or top / bottom). One side shows the problematic state, one shows the resolved state. Xiaohei may appear in one or both.

**Key rule:** The contrast must be visually obvious without reading the labels.

---

## 4. Character State

**Use when:** The article describes an internal state, emotion, or cognitive condition.

**Structure:** Xiaohei occupies the scene in a specific physical posture that embodies the state. The environment reinforces the mood.

**Key rule:** Use physical metaphors (Xiaohei buried under objects = overwhelmed; Xiaohei holding a single clear object = focused).

---

## 5. Conceptual Metaphor

**Use when:** The article makes an abstract argument that benefits from a physical analogy.

**Structure:** Invent a low-tech physical scenario that maps onto the abstract concept. Xiaohei enacts the metaphor.

**Key rule:** The metaphor must be fresh — don't reuse standard metaphors (conveyor belt, funnel, ladder). Reinvent from the article's specific content.

---

## 6. Method / Layers

**Use when:** The article describes a framework, model, or layered approach.

**Structure:** Horizontal layers or stacked zones with Xiaohei working within or between them.

**Key rule:** Avoid looking like a formal pyramid chart. Keep it loose and sketch-like.

---

## 7. Map / Journey

**Use when:** The article describes a path, progression, or decision journey.

**Structure:** A winding or branching path with waypoints. Xiaohei is on the path at a meaningful position.

**Key rule:** The path should feel exploratory, not like a formal decision tree.

---

## 8. Comic Strip / Panels

**Use when:** The article tells a story, describes a sequence of events, or uses narrative.

**Structure:** 2–4 mini panels arranged in a grid or strip. Each panel is a moment in the sequence. Xiaohei appears across panels.

**Key rule:** Keep each panel extremely simple — one action, no background detail.

---

## Creation Method (3 Steps)

For any concept, follow this process:

1. **Convert abstract → physical action**
   "Stuck" → Xiaohei physically wedged between objects
   "Settling" → objects slowly sinking to the bottom
   "Opening up" → Xiaohei pushing open a door or hatch

2. **Replace digital/system concepts with low-tech objects**
   Database → filing cabinet or stack of folders
   Algorithm → a sorting machine or mechanical sorter
   Network → a web of strings or tubes

3. **Give Xiaohei a specific action**
   Not "Xiaohei stands near the system"
   But "Xiaohei cranks the handle that runs the sorting machine"

---

## Originality Rule

**Every image must reinvent the metaphor from the current article.**

Do not reuse compositions from previous work. Old fallbacks to avoid:
- Conveyor belt with items being sorted
- Funnel filtering objects
- Ladder with Xiaohei climbing
- Venn diagram zones

If you find yourself defaulting to one of these, stop and invent something new.
